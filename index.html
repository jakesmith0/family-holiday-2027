Where could we go? (areas)
  • Vale of Pickering · leading
    An inland farm base with a pool and playroom. Coast day trips 30–45 min away to Scarborough, Filey and Whitby. Pickering itself is a small market town.
  • Filey / Scarborough / Cayton · in-play
    A proper seaside holiday: flat promenades, sandy beaches, Sea Life, soft play. Scarborough is busy and practical; Filey is gentle and quiet.
  • Bridlington / Flamborough · in-play
    A traditional seaside town with a flat promenade and harbour. Sewerby Hall and Flamborough Head are on the doorstep, with Wolds villages behind.
  • Swaledale (Yorkshire Dales) · in-play
    A cosy house in a pretty dale with a pub, riverside strolls and Richmond nearby.
  • North Norfolk (Weybourne–Cromer) · in-play
    Pool mornings on site, then promenade walks with chips at Cromer or Sheringham, Pensthorpe's indoor play barn, and a pub supper in the village.
  • Northumberland coast · in-play
    Huge beaches, castles, pretty villages, and drives between them at a relaxed pace.
  • North Wales coast (Prestatyn–Llandudno) · in-play
    Seaside towns with piers and trams, a zoo, and castles. Chester Zoo is under an hour away.
  • Center Parcs (Woburn / Sherwood) · in-play
    Resort in the woods: subtropical pool, soft play, everything on site.
  • Whitby / Sandsend · parked
    The most atmospheric town on the coast, with the abbey, harbour and a beach at Sandsend.
  • Peak District (Bakewell / Carsington) · parked
    Market towns, flat buggy trails, farm parks and a pool on site. No sea.
  • Holmfirth (South Pennines) · parked
    A hillside house with a big garden. Quiet, no sea.
  • Anglesey · parked
    Beach villages (Rhosneigr), Beaumaris castle and seafront, the Sea Zoo.
  • Pembrokeshire / Cardigan · parked
    Purpose-built toddler complexes: pool, play barn, animal feeding.
  • West Cumbria / Lakes · parked
    Lakes and fells. Pretty, but walking-led in February.
  • Dorset / East Devon coast · parked
    Jurassic Coast villages, fossil beaches, milder weather.
